# D11 train-station source screen — 2026-10-04

The first-party [DutchEnglish.com travel-phrases lesson](https://dutchenglish.com/lessons/dutch-travel-phrases)
was directly inspected in the browser. The lesson identifies itself as
A2 language vocabulary, and the [publisher's lesson index](https://dutchenglish.com/lessons/)
identifies its A1–B2 lesson bands as CEFR levels. Its “Transportation” section uses singular
`treinstation` in a request for the station's location and translates
the phrase as a train-station request. The target form and sense match
the exact `candidate-treinstation` hash. This is one preliminary
contextual-use observation, with reference-only rights under the
site's all-rights-reserved footer. The publisher's self-declared
lesson level is an exposure signal, not an adjudicated word level.

The [Lingua.com reading index](https://lingua.com/nl/nederlands/lezen/)
still lists its _De weg naar het treinstation_ story under A2 while
the [PDF](https://lingua.com/pdf/nederlands-tekst-weg-vragen.pdf)
labels the same story B1. This separate publisher conflict is not
resolved by the DutchEnglish.com observation; no Lingua row was added.

The directly inspected [CED-Groep Nieuwsrekenen context sheet](https://www.nieuwsbegrip.nl/sites/default/files/download-files/contextkraker%20niveau%20A2.pdf)
also uses `treinstation` on PDF page 8 and prints “niveau A2”.
However, the publisher's [Nieuwsrekenen explanation](https://www.nieuwsbegrip.nl/nieuwsrekenen-stappenplan)
identifies its AA/A1/A2/B1/B2/C bands as levels for school
mathematics context problems and calculation skills. It does not
establish that this A2 is the CEFR language level. The identical
letters are insufficient, so this occurrence stays outside the
graded-language ledger. No source text was copied into the dataset.
